

## 1. Games that want players to collaborate and build community

This is your strongest audience segment.

These games benefit from Kiubo because **real‑world encounters reinforce in‑game social bonds**.

### Examples of genres that fit:
- **Cozy multiplayer** (Animal Crossing‑like, Stardew‑like)
- **MMOs** (FFXIV, WoW, Guild Wars)
- **Survival co‑op** (Valheim, Rust, Palworld)
- **Social RPGs** (Genshin, Honkai, Pokémon‑likes)
- **City builders with social layers** (SimCity BuildIt, Township)

### Why they care:
- Kiubo creates **micro‑moments of connection**.
- Encourages **community rituals** (“I met 3 guildmates today”).
- Adds **low‑pressure socialization** without chat toxicity.

### Trend alignment:
- “Ambient social play” is rising (Fortnite Festival, cozy games, Roblox hangouts).
- Players want **connection without commitment**.



## 2. Games that want to keep players engaged even when they’re not playing

This is the “background engagement” segment

### Examples:
- **Idle games**
- **Live service games**
- **Gacha / RPGs**
- **Fitness games**
- **AR games**
- **Mobile companion apps for PC/console titles**

### Why they care:
- Kiubo becomes a **daily ritual**.
- Encourages **habit loops** (“check your Kiubo encounters”).
- Adds **offline progression** without needing to open the game.

### Trend alignment:
- Pokémon GO’s Adventure Sync proved that **passive engagement = retention**.
- Live service games need **daily touchpoints** that aren’t grindy.

Kiubo gives them a new retention mechanic.

### Trend alignment:

Kiubo becomes a **bridge between physical gatherings and digital rewards**.
